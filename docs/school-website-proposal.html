1. Why the school needs this website

The school already runs a complete management system: admissions, classes, marks, report cards, fees through the ledger, transport, boarding and the library. What it does not yet have is a front door — a public place where a parent who has never met the school can find it, judge it, ask a question and start an application without picking up the phone.

Today every enquiry arrives as a phone call or a walk-in during office hours. Each one costs staff time, arrives without a record, and is lost the moment the call ends. A website turns that traffic into something the school owns:

What happens todayWhat the website changes
A parent phones to ask about fees, the bus route or when the term starts.They read it on the site at 9 pm, and only call when they are ready to apply.
An enquiry is written on a notepad and followed up if someone remembers.The enquiry lands in the system as a record with a name, a phone number and a status, and is followed up because it appears on a list.
An application form is collected from the gate, filled by hand, and re-typed by the registrar.The parent applies online; the application appears in the Admissions pipeline ready for review — no re-typing, no lost forms.
Parents ask the office for a fee balance or a report card.They sign in to the portal and see it themselves, any time.
Newsletters and notices are printed and sent home with the child.They are published once and reach every parent by web, e-mail and SMS.
A prospective parent Googles the school and finds nothing, or a Facebook page from 2019.The school is the first, authoritative result — and is the source that AI assistants quote when asked about it.

The single most important principle in this document

The website is not a second system. It is a public face on the system the school already runs. Term dates, fee structures, notices, transport routes and the admissions pipeline all live in the management system; the website reads from it and writes back into it. Anything that is typed twice will, sooner or later, disagree with itself.

Objectives, in the order they matter

  1. Convert enquiries into applications. Every page should make the next step obvious: enquire, book a tour, or apply.
  2. Answer the repeated questions — fees, term dates, uniform, transport, entry requirements — so the office answers fewer of them.
  3. Serve the parents the school already has: notices, calendar, results, fee statements, payments.
  4. Present the school honestly and well to families, to the Ministry, and to prospective staff.
  5. Be legible to machines — search engines and AI assistants — because that is increasingly how parents find a school.

4. Page-by-page functionality

Priority key: P1 launch   P2 soon after   P3 later   AI machine-readable content.

4.1 Home P1

A visitor decides in about eight seconds whether to keep reading. The home page must answer what kind of school is this, for which ages, where, and what do I do next — above the fold, before any scrolling.

  • Hero — one strong photograph of real children on the real campus, the school name and motto (“Knowledge, Character, Excellence”), and two buttons: Apply Online and Book a Tour.
  • Quick-answer strip — Pre-Primary to Junior Secondary · Day & Boarding · CBC curriculum · Kiambu Road, Nairobi. Four facts, no prose.
  • Why families choose us — four to six cards, each a claim with evidence (class size, teacher qualifications, results, facilities).
  • Levels at a glance — four tiles linking to the level pages, each with the age range and a one-line promise.
  • Latest news & notices AI — three most recent items, pulled live from the MIS announcements so nobody has to copy them.
  • Upcoming dates — next three calendar events, from the MIS academic calendar.
  • Parent Portal — a prominent panel: check fees, results, attendance. Signed-in parents should never have to hunt for it.
  • Proof — parent and alumni testimonials with names and photos (with consent), plus accreditation marks.
  • Closing call to action — enquiry form inline (name, phone, child’s grade) so nobody has to navigate to contact us.
  • Footer — address, map, phone, e-mail, office hours, paybill, social links, policy links.

4.2 About & governance P1

  • Our story and mission — founding, philosophy, values, what the school is deliberately not.
  • Leadership — Principal’s welcome (photo, signature, 200 words), Deputy/Registrar, Board of Directors or Trustees, PTA chair.
  • Our team — teaching staff with photo, subject and qualification; support staff by department. Can be generated from HR records; individual consent required before a photo is published.
  • Accreditation & registration AI — Ministry registration MOE/PS/2016/1187, licence PRI/2016/00417, KNEC centre number, associations. Parents and the Ministry both look for these.
  • Campus & facilities — classrooms, science and computer labs, library, dining hall, dormitories, playing fields, sick bay, bus fleet; photographs and a short walkthrough video.
  • Safeguarding statement — child protection policy, staff vetting, visitor rules, CCTV, gate procedure. Increasingly the first thing a careful parent reads.

4.3 Academics P1

  • One page per level (Pre-Primary, Lower Primary, Upper Primary, Junior Secondary): ages, typical day, learning areas, class sizes, assessment, transition to the next level, the teachers, the fees for that level, and a call to apply.
  • Subjects & electives — the learning areas offered, which are core and which are chosen, drawn from the subject list in the MIS so the website cannot drift from what is actually taught.
  • Assessment & reporting (CBC) — how continuous assessment, exams and competency bands work; what a report card looks like (sample, anonymised); when results are published; how parents access them (link to the portal).
  • Academic calendar AI — term dates, half-terms, exam weeks, closing and opening days, published straight from the MIS. One source, one truth. Downloadable as PDF and subscribable as a calendar feed (.ics) so it lands in a parent’s phone.
  • Results & achievements — school-level performance, national exam outcomes, competition results. Never individual pupils’ marks.
  • Homework & reading policy, book lists per grade (PDF).

4.4 Admissions P1 — the commercial heart of the site

Treat this as the school’s shop window. Everything else exists to bring a parent here.

  • How to apply — four numbered steps with realistic timelines: 1. Enquire or tour → 2. Apply online → 3. Assessment & interview → 4. Offer, acceptance and reporting day.
  • Entry requirements per grade — age on 1 January, documents required (birth certificate, previous report, transfer letter, immunisation card, NEMIS/assessment number, passport photos), what a transfer needs.
  • Apply online — the form itself (section 6 and Appendix A). Saves progress, accepts document uploads, works on a phone.
  • Intakes & deadlines — when the school takes new pupils, when places typically fill, waiting-list policy.
  • Fees for applicants — full fee structure, what is included, what is extra, payment plans, the application fee and whether it is refundable.
  • Scholarships & bursaries — what exists, who qualifies, how to apply, deadline, and how decisions are communicated.
  • Book a tour — a date/time picker showing the school’s open slots; confirmation by e-mail and SMS; a reminder the day before; the visit recorded in the MIS.
  • Admissions FAQ AI — twenty real questions with direct answers. This page will attract more search traffic than any other and is what AI assistants will quote.
  • Talk to the registrar — name, photo, direct line, WhatsApp, and office hours. Admissions is a human decision; parents want a person.

4.5 Fees & payments P1

  • Published fee structure per level and per term: tuition, boarding, lunch, transport, activity, examination and one-off charges, with a clear note on what is compulsory and what is optional. Generated from the MIS fee structure, so the published figure is the figure that will be invoiced.
  • Payment options — M-Pesa paybill 522533 with the child’s admission number as the account, bank transfer details, banker’s cheque, and the instalment plan.
  • Fee calculator P2 — pick a grade, boarder or day, bus route and options; see the termly and annual total. Removes the most common phone call the bursar receives.
  • Discounts — sibling discount, early-payment discount, bursaries: stated plainly with the conditions.
  • Pay now — a button that takes a signed-in parent to the portal, where an M-Pesa STK push can be sent to their own phone for their own child.
  • Fee policy — due dates, what happens when fees are late, refunds on withdrawal, and who to talk to about difficulty paying.

4.6 Student life P2

  • A day at Green Valley — an hour-by-hour walkthrough for a day scholar and for a boarder. Parents picture their own child in it.
  • Clubs & societies, sports, music, drama and arts — what is offered per term, who runs it, photographs, and results.
  • Boarding life — the houses, dormitories and beds, the warden, the weekly routine, prep times, visiting days, exeat rules, what to pack (downloadable list), meals and the menu cycle.
  • Counselling & wellbeing — the counsellor, the sick bay and nurse, medical procedures, how a parent is contacted in an emergency, the anti-bullying policy.
  • Student leadership — the council, prefects, service and community projects.

4.7 School services — transport, boarding, library P2

These three pages exist because the management system already holds the data; publishing them costs almost nothing and answers a great many phone calls.

  • Transport AI — every route with its stops, pick-up and drop-off times, the termly fare, the buses and their capacity, and the rules (a child is released only to a named adult, a change of stop needs a written request). Fed live from the transport module, so a route change is published the moment the transport office makes it.
  • Boarding — the houses, what a dormitory looks like, the kit list, laundry, prep and lights-out, visiting days.
  • Library — opening hours, borrowing rules (how many books, for how long, what a late return costs), the reading programme, and P3 a public catalogue search so a pupil can check whether a title is on the shelf before walking over.

4.8 News, notices & events P1 AI

  • News — articles with a photograph, author, date and category; tags per level so a Grade 3 parent can filter to what concerns them.
  • Notices to parents — published from the MIS announcements. Write once in the system; it reaches the website, e-mail and SMS together.
  • Events calendar — month and list views, each event with date, time, venue, audience, and where relevant an RSVP; downloadable .ics and subscribable.
  • Newsletters — a browsable archive of termly newsletters as PDFs.
  • Achievements — academic, sporting and arts results as they happen. This is the content that makes a school look alive.
  • Emergency banner — a red strip across every page for a closure, a change of reporting date or a security notice, switched on from the MIS in one click. Rarely used, indispensable when needed.

4.9 Gallery & media P2

  • Albums by event and term; images compressed and served responsively so they open quickly on a phone with a weak signal.
  • Video: campus tour, sports day, music festival, the Principal’s welcome.
  • Consent register — no child’s photograph is published without a recorded parental consent, and the consent flag lives on the pupil’s record in the MIS. A parent can withdraw consent at any time and the image comes down.
  • A virtual 360° tour P3 — valuable for families abroad or upcountry.

4.10 Careers P2

  • Current vacancies with role, subject, level, contract type, closing date and the full description.
  • An online application form with CV and certificate upload, feeding the HR recruitment list rather than an inbox.
  • Why work here: professional development, benefits, staff testimonials.
  • A speculative-application form for when there are no vacancies, and TSC/child-protection requirements stated up front.

4.11 Contact & enquiries P1

  • Address, an embedded map with driving directions and a landmark, office hours, and the main lines.
  • Direct lines by department — Admissions, Bursar, Transport, Boarding, Head Teacher — so a caller reaches the right desk first time.
  • Enquiry form (section 7) — the most important form on the site after the application.
  • WhatsApp business link, because that is how most Kenyan parents prefer to ask a quick question.
  • A map of where the school buses run, for families deciding whether the location works.

4.12 Downloads & policies P1

One page, everything a parent is ever asked to “collect from the office”: fee structure, term dates, uniform and kit lists, book lists, boarding packing list, medical form, consent forms, transport request, transfer request, privacy policy, safeguarding policy, anti-bullying policy, code of conduct, parent handbook.

6. Online admissions, end to end

This is the single highest-value function on the website. The flow below is designed so that a parent on a phone, with a patchy connection, can finish it in one sitting — and so the registrar never re-types anything.

The parent’s journey

  1. Start — grade applying for, intake term and year, how they heard about the school.
  2. The child — names, date of birth (with an immediate check against the grade’s age requirement), gender, nationality, current school, last grade completed, day or boarding, any medical or learning needs.
  3. Parents / guardians — primary guardian’s name, relationship, phone, e-mail, ID number, occupation, address; a second guardian optional; the phone number is verified with an SMS code, which alone removes most junk applications.
  4. Documents — birth certificate, most recent report card, transfer letter, immunisation card, passport photograph. Uploaded as photos from the phone; the form says plainly which are required now and which may follow.
  5. Options — transport (with the route and stop chosen from the live route list), boarding, lunch, electives where relevant.
  6. Declaration & consent — truth of information, acceptance of the data-protection notice, consent (or refusal) for photography.
  7. Application fee P2 — optional M-Pesa STK push at submission; the receipt is attached to the application.
  8. Submit — the parent receives an application number on screen, by SMS and by e-mail, with what happens next and when to expect it.

What happens on the school’s side

  • The application appears in Admissions in the management system as New, with its documents attached — no re-keying.
  • The registrar moves it through the pipeline: New → Under review → Assessment/Interview booked → Offered → Accepted → Admitted (or Waiting list / Declined).
  • Each move can notify the parent by SMS and e-mail automatically, with the message text kept in the system rather than in someone’s head.
  • On Admit, the system creates the pupil, issues the admission number, opens the fee account, places the child in a class, and — where chosen — assigns the bus route and the hostel bed. The website form and the school roll are the same record from beginning to end.

Design rules that decide whether the form is finished or abandoned

  • Four short steps with a visible progress bar, never one long page.
  • Everything saves as it is typed; a resume link is sent by SMS so an interrupted parent can return.
  • Ask only what is needed to decide. Everything else can be collected at reporting.
  • Phone-number and date fields formatted for Kenya; the whole form usable one-handed on a mid-range Android.
  • Uploads accept photographs of documents, not just scans, and compress them client-side.
  • Plain-language errors next to the field, never a single failure message at the top.
  • A printable version for the rare parent who still prefers paper — the same fields, so the registrar can type it in once.

7. Enquiries: the form that must never lose a lead

Most visitors are not ready to apply. The enquiry form captures them anyway, and its only job is to be short.

  • Five fields: parent’s name, phone, e-mail (optional), child’s grade of interest, and the question. Anything more reduces completion.
  • It appears on the home page, the admissions page, every level page and the contact page — the same form, remembering which page it came from.
  • On submit: an immediate thank-you with the expected response time, an SMS confirmation to the parent, an instant notification to the admissions desk, and a record created in the system with a status of New.
  • Enquiries are worked as a list — New → Contacted → Tour booked → Applied → Enrolled → Lost — so the school can see, at the end of a term, how many enquiries the website produced and how many became pupils.
  • Spam is kept out with an invisible check rather than a puzzle a parent has to solve.
  • An enquiry that goes untouched for 48 hours should raise a flag. A lead left for a week is a lead lost to another school.

Rules for the join

  • The public interface is read-only except for applications, enquiries and job applications, each of which is rate-limited and validated.
  • It exposes no personal data — no pupil names, no marks, no balances. Those live behind the portal sign-in.
  • Website pages are cached for a few minutes; notices and the emergency banner refresh immediately on publish.
  • If the management system is briefly unreachable the website still serves its last good copy. A parent must never see an error because an internal system is restarting.

9. Making the site AI-friendly and findable

Parents increasingly ask an AI assistant before they ask Google, and assistants answer from sites they can parse. A site built as below is quoted accurately; a site of images and PDFs is skipped or, worse, guessed about.

9.1 Structured data

  • School on the home page: legal name, registration numbers, address with coordinates, phone, e-mail, curriculum, ages served, opening hours (Appendix B).
  • FAQPage on the admissions and fees FAQs — the format assistants quote most readily.
  • Event on every calendar entry; NewsArticle on every article; JobPosting on every vacancy; BreadcrumbList site-wide.
  • A sitemap.xml that updates itself, and an llms.txt at the root summarising the school and pointing to the pages an assistant should read first.

9.2 Writing for both readers

  • Every fact a parent might ask for exists as text, not only inside an image or a PDF. A fee structure that exists only as a scan is invisible to search and to assistants.
  • One clear question per heading — “How much are fees for Grade 7?” outperforms “Financial Information”.
  • Dates written in full (“Term 2 begins on Monday 4 May 2026”), never “next term”.
  • Each page states plainly who it is for and what to do next.
  • Descriptive alt text on every image; real captions, not file names.

9.3 A school assistant on the website P2

A chat box, bottom-right, that answers from the school’s own published content — fees, term dates, entry requirements, routes, uniform — and nothing else. It must:

  • Answer only from the website and the public MIS data, and say “I don’t know, let me connect you to the office” rather than invent;
  • Offer to capture an enquiry when it cannot answer, so the conversation still becomes a lead;
  • Never discuss an individual pupil — that belongs behind the portal sign-in;
  • Hand over to WhatsApp or a phone number during office hours;
  • Keep a transcript the admissions office can read, which is also the best possible list of what the website should explain better.

9.4 Search visibility

  • Target the phrases parents actually type: “private school Kiambu Road”, “CBC junior secondary Nairobi boarding”, “school fees Grade 7 Nairobi”.
  • A Google Business Profile with photographs, hours and reviews, consistent with the site to the letter.
  • Fast on a phone: under three seconds on 3G, images compressed, nothing that blocks the first paint.
  • Correct titles and descriptions per page, and social preview cards so a shared link looks like the school and not a bare URL.

10. Accessibility, language and inclusion

  • WCAG 2.2 AA as the standard: contrast, keyboard navigation, focus that is visible, labelled forms, captioned video, headings in order.
  • Phone first. Most parents will only ever see the site on a phone; design for that screen and let the desktop follow.
  • Low bandwidth. Pages must be usable on a slow connection — compressed images, no autoplaying video, no heavy fonts.
  • Language. English throughout, with Kiswahili for the pages that matter most to every parent — admissions, fees, term dates, contact. P2
  • Print. Fee structure, calendar and application pages must print cleanly on A4 — many parents still keep a folder.
  • Plain language. Short sentences, no internal jargon, no acronym without its expansion.

13. Technical approach, hosting and cost

  • Same technology as the management system (Next.js, TypeScript, PostgreSQL) so one team maintains both and the join between them is direct rather than bolted on.
  • Content management — school staff edit through a simple admin screen, not by editing code. Pages, news, events, gallery and downloads are all editable; layout and structure are not.
  • Hosting — the same platform as the MIS, with a global CDN, automatic HTTPS and daily backups. No server for the school to look after.
  • Domain — greenvalley.ac.ke for the website and portal.greenvalley.ac.ke for the portal, with e-mail on the school’s own domain.
  • Performance budget — first meaningful paint under 2.5 s on 3G; every image compressed and sized; the site works with JavaScript disabled for the pages that only present information.
  • Environments — a staging site the school reviews before anything reaches the public one.

14. Security, privacy and child protection

  • Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019 — a plain-English privacy notice, a lawful basis for every form, a named data-protection contact, and a retention rule for unsuccessful applications (suggested: 12 months, then deletion).
  • Children’s data is treated as sensitive. No pupil name, photograph, mark or balance is ever public; all of it sits behind the portal sign-in.
  • Photographic consent is recorded per child and is respected by the gallery automatically; withdrawal takes effect immediately.
  • Forms are rate-limited, validated on the server, protected from spam, and their uploads are scanned and type-restricted.
  • Transport — HTTPS everywhere, HSTS, secure headers, no third-party script that has not been reviewed.
  • Payments — the school never handles card data; M-Pesa is initiated from the portal and confirmed by Safaricom’s callback.
  • Safeguarding — no staff or pupil personal phone numbers published; a clear route for reporting a concern; the safeguarding policy public.
  • Backups and recovery — daily, tested, with a stated recovery time; and an annual review of who still has editing access.

Appendix C — Decisions needed from the school

  1. Domain — confirm greenvalley.ac.ke is registered in the school’s name, and agree the portal address.
  2. Fee publication — publish the full fee structure openly, or show it only after an enquiry? (Recommendation: publish. Parents distrust a school that hides its fees, and the office fields fewer calls.)
  3. Application fee — charged at submission, or on acceptance of an offer?
  4. Photography consent — the wording, and whether existing pupils need to be re-consented before launch.
  5. Who owns the website — name the person responsible for content, and the person who approves publication.
  6. Kiswahili pages — required at launch, or phase 6?
  7. Website assistant — approve in principle, and agree what it must never discuss.
  8. Staff directory — publish photographs and qualifications, or names and roles only?
  9. Tour slots — which days and times the school will commit to.
  10. Response-time promise — what the site tells a parent to expect after an enquiry. (One working day is credible and achievable.)
